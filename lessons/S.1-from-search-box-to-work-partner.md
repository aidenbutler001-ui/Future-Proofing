## Read
You have probably used an AI chatbot to look up a fact, settle an argument, or make an image. That is a fine start, and it is also the weakest way to use one. This course is about the stronger way: using AI to get real work done.

**Two habits to change.**

1. **Facts are the weakest use.** A chatbot writes what sounds right. It can be wrong in the same confident tone as when it is right (lesson 1.2 explains why). For a random fact, check it. For something that matters, don't rely on it alone.
2. **A one-line question is the weakest way to ask.** The assistant knows nothing about you, your goal or your situation unless you say so.

**A better picture: a capable new colleague.** Imagine someone who has read an enormous amount, writes quickly, never gets tired, and has just walked into the room knowing nothing about you. You would not say "Help with my presentation". You would say who it is for, what you need, how long it is, and what good looks like. Then you would read what they gave you and ask for changes. That is the whole skill:

> **Brief it. Read it critically. Ask for changes.**

**What assistants are good at (the work partner uses):**
- First drafts of emails, summaries, outlines, plans
- Rewriting: shorter, friendlier, clearer, in a different tone
- Explaining something at your level, with examples
- Organizing messy notes into a structure
- Brainstorming options and listing pros and cons
- Checking your work and pointing out gaps
- Practicing: quizzing you, role-playing an interview

**What to be careful with:**
- Exact facts, numbers, quotes and sources
- Anything where a mistake is costly and hard to spot
- Anything private (the next lessons set the rules)

**You stay in charge.** The assistant does the legwork. You decide the goal, judge the result, and take responsibility for what you use.

## Example
Search-box use: "how do I ask for a deadline extension"
Work-partner use: "I'm a second-year student. My lab report is due Friday and I'm two days behind because of a family matter. Draft a polite, short email to my professor asking for a three-day extension. Don't over-explain, and offer to send what I have so far."
The second gets an email you can send after a quick edit.

## Puzzle: Upgrade the question
You get 5 search-box style questions ("best way to study", "write about climate", ...). Rewrite each as a brief that gives the goal, your situation and what good looks like.
- Goal: each rewrite includes the three elements, in 4 of 5 cases.
- Hidden tests: the simulated replies to your rewrites are scored for usefulness against the original questions' replies.
- Grader: rules check for the three elements; a rubric scores how much more useful the reply is. Feedback names the missing element.

## Hints
1. Who is it for, and what will they do with the result?
2. What would you tell a new colleague first?

## Reflect
What have you used AI for so far? Which of those uses would a colleague-style brief improve?
