## Read
You have done one task. Now look wider. A **task audit** is a short list of the things you do repeatedly, with a note on where AI could help. It does three things: it shows you where the biggest time savings are, it gives you your own practice material for the rest of the course, and it ends with an automation you build yourself (lesson 6.6).

**Make your list.** Write down 10 recurring tasks from the last two weeks. Include study or work, admin, communication, planning and personal tasks. Examples: replying to emails, taking notes in class or meetings, summarizing readings, writing weekly updates, organizing files, planning meals, tracking applications, preparing for exams.

**Score each task** on four questions (high, medium, low):
1. **How often?** Daily tasks matter more than rare ones.
2. **How long does it take?**
3. **How routine is it?** Same steps each time is easier to hand off.
4. **How costly is a mistake?** Low cost means safer to try first.

**Mark each task** with a first guess at the help it could use: *draft*, *rewrite*, *summarize*, *organize*, *plan*, *explain*, *check*, *practice* or *none*.

**Pick three to work on first:** frequent, routine, low-risk. Save the list. You will come back to it after Modules 2, 4 and 6, moving tasks from "idea" to "assistant helps" to "automated".

**A note on judgement.** The best tasks to hand off are not always the most boring ones. Some work is where you learn the most, such as writing an argument in your own words for a class. Decide on purpose what to keep doing yourself (lesson 7.4 returns to this for school and work).

## Example
A part-time worker lists: weekly shift swap emails (weekly, 15 min, routine, low risk: draft), notes after team calls (3 times a week, 20 min, medium routine, medium risk: summarize), course reading summaries (weekly, 2 hours, medium routine, low risk: summarize and quiz), and tax paperwork (yearly, high risk: none for now). Her first three: shift emails, course readings, call notes.

## Puzzle: Pick the first three
You get a sample audit of 10 tasks with scores. Pick the three best to start with and say why, and mark one task that should stay human-only.
- Goal: your three picks are frequent, routine and low-risk, and your human-only pick has a good reason.
- Hidden tests: two different sample audits.
- Grader: key with accepted choices and a rubric on the reasons.

## Hints
1. Which tasks come up often and follow the same steps?
2. Which task would cause real trouble if the result were wrong?

## Reflect
Write your own list of 10. Which three will you start with?
