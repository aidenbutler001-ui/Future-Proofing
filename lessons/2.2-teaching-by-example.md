## Read
Describing a pattern can take paragraphs. Showing two or three examples often does it better. This is called **few-shot prompting**.

Good examples:
- **Cover the range.** Include the easy case and the awkward one.
- **Match the output format exactly**, since the model copies it.
- **Are consistent.** If two examples disagree, the model guesses.

Watch for **copying**: if every example is positive, the model leans positive. Balance them.

## Example
Task: label customer messages as Billing, Bug or Other.
Examples:
- "I was charged twice" → Billing
- "The export button does nothing" → Bug
- "Do you have a student discount?" → Other

## Puzzle: Sort the support tickets
Write a prompt with at most 4 examples that makes a simulated model label 10 hidden tickets correctly. The tickets include tricky ones (a billing problem caused by a bug, a polite message with an angry subject).

- Goal: 8 of 10 correct.
- Hidden tests: 10 tickets, shown after the run, never before.
- Grader: exact label match. Feedback shows which tickets failed and what kind they were.

## Hints
1. Which kind of ticket does your prompt not show?
2. Add the example that looks like your failure.

## Reflect
What's the cost of one more example? What does it buy?
