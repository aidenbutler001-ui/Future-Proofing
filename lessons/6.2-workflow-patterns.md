## Read
Most workflows are assembled from five patterns.

1. **Chain:** step A feeds step B. Draft the email, then check its tone.
2. **Route:** classify the input first, then send it down the right path. Billing question goes to one prompt, bug report to another.
3. **Parallelize:** run independent pieces at once and combine them. Review three documents together, or ask the same question three ways and compare.
4. **Check and revise:** one step produces, another critiques against a rubric, and the first revises. Stop after a set number of rounds.
5. **Delegate:** a lead step splits a job into pieces for helpers (lesson 5.3) when you can't know the pieces beforehand.

Add **plain code** between model steps. Sorting, math, formatting and database lookups are better done by ordinary code than by a model.

Design tip: write the workflow as a diagram of boxes and arrows first, with the input and output of each box. If a box's output isn't something the next box can use, fix that before building.

## Puzzle: Build the pipeline
You get a business request: "Turn incoming support emails into tickets with a category, urgency and a draft reply." Arrange boxes (classify, extract fields, draft reply, check reply, human review, create ticket) into a pipeline using the patterns.
- Goal: pass 5 of 6 hidden sample emails end to end in the simulator.
- Hidden tests: 6 emails, including a spam message, one with two issues, and one that needs a person.
- Grader: simulator runs the pipeline; feedback shows where an email took the wrong path.

## Hints
1. What should happen to the spam message?
2. Where does a human need to see the result before it leaves?
