## Read
One agent doing everything fills its context and gets confused. A harness can start **sub-agents**: separate model runs with their own instructions, tools and fresh context. A lead agent hands off a piece of the work and receives a short result.

When splitting helps:
- Pieces are **independent** (research three companies at once).
- A piece needs **different tools or permissions** (a read-only reviewer).
- A piece makes lots of noisy output you don't want in the main context.

When it hurts:
- Pieces depend on each other.
- The hand-off loses key details. The brief you give a sub-agent is a prompt, and it knows only what the brief says.

A good brief states the goal, what's known, what to return, and what not to do.

**Opsec check.** Each sub-agent receives a brief, which may contain sensitive data, and may have its own tools. Give each only what its part needs, and check what it returns before passing it on.

## Puzzle: Write the hand-off
A lead agent must produce a one-page comparison of three suppliers. You write the brief for the sub-agent that researches one supplier.
- Goal: three sub-agent runs return results the lead can compare side by side.
- Hidden tests: three suppliers with different amounts of public information; one has none.
- Grader: rules check each return for the required fields and for honest "not found" values.

## Hints
1. What format makes three results easy to compare?
2. What should a sub-agent do when it finds nothing?
