## Read
A model knows general patterns from training, but not your contract, your notes or this week's report. To use them, put the text in the context (lesson 1.5) and tell the model how to use it.

Habits that help:
- **Mark the material clearly.** Wrap it in labels such as `<document name="lease.pdf"> ... </document>` so the model can tell your instructions from the text.
- **Put the instruction near the question,** and say what to rely on: "Use only the document below."
- **Ask for the quote.** "Give the sentence that supports each answer." A quote can be found in the text; an unsupported claim can't.
- **Allow "not in the document."** If you don't, the model may fill gaps from general knowledge and sound equally sure.

This is called **grounding**: tying answers to given material. It reduces made-up facts (lesson 1.6) but doesn't remove the need to check.

## Example
Weak: "What's the notice period?" (with a lease attached)
Better: "Using only the lease between the tags, state the notice period for ending the tenancy. Quote the clause. If the lease doesn't say, reply 'Not stated.'"

**Opsec check.** Putting a document in front of a model is uploading it. Classify the document first (lesson 0.1). Sensitive or restricted documents need an approved tool and permission. Strip metadata, comments and tracked changes. Remember that a document can contain hidden instructions aimed at the model (lesson 7.2).

## Puzzle: Stay inside the document
You get a 1-page policy and a simulated model. Write the instruction so the model answers 6 hidden questions from the policy only. Two questions can't be answered from it.
- Goal: 4 answerable questions answered with a correct quote; both unanswerable ones reported as not stated.
- Hidden tests: the 6 questions.
- Grader: rules check the answer, that the quote appears in the policy, and "not stated" for the unanswerable ones.
