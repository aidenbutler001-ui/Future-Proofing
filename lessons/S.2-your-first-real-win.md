## Read
This lesson is about doing, not reading. You will complete one real task in about 15 minutes.

**First, a safe-start rule.** The full data-safety lesson comes right after this one. Until then, use only material you would be comfortable posting publicly. No passwords, no account or ID numbers, no other people's private details, nothing you were told to keep private. Pick a task where this is easy.

**Pick a small, real task you are about to do anyway.** Good first tasks:
- Write an email or message you have been putting off
- Plan a study week or a weekend
- Turn your own messy notes into a clear checklist
- Explain a concept from class in plain words, then quiz you on it
- Compare two options for a decision (a laptop, two courses) and list the trade-offs
- Make a packing list, a meal plan, or a schedule with constraints

**The five steps.**
1. **Write a brief** with the goal, your situation, any limits (length, tone), and what a good result looks like.
2. **Read the first answer critically.** What is useful? What is wrong or missing? Do the facts hold up?
3. **Ask for specific changes.** "Shorter." "Less formal." "Add a second option." "You assumed X; I meant Y." One or two rounds is normal.
4. **Check anything that matters** (dates, names, numbers, claims) against a real source.
5. **Use it, edit it, own it.** Make it sound like you.

**Compare.** Note how long it took compared with doing it alone, and what you would change next time. Don't expect perfection. Expect a strong first draft and a faster finish.

## Example
Task: a student needs a revision plan for three exams. Brief: "I have exams on Tuesday (chemistry), Thursday (statistics) and next Monday (history). I can study 2 hours on weekdays and 4 on weekends. I'm weakest in statistics. Make a day-by-day plan until Monday, with a short list of topics per session." The first answer puts history too late. The student replies "Move history earlier and add one rest evening," and checks the exam dates against the timetable.

## Puzzle: Fix the first answer
You are shown a brief and an assistant's first answer (simulated). The answer has a useful structure, one wrong assumption, and one detail that needs checking. Write your follow-up message.
- Goal: your follow-up corrects the wrong assumption, asks for one specific change, and names the detail you will verify.
- Hidden tests: two different task scenarios.
- Grader: rubric checks the three elements; feedback shows the follow-up that would have worked.

## Hints
1. What did the assistant assume that wasn't true?
2. Which detail would cost you if it were wrong?

## Reflect
How did the result compare with what you would have done alone? What would you try next?
