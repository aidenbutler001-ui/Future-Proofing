## Read
Lesson 1.3 described a model producing its reply one token at a time. A **reasoning model** is trained and run so that it first produces a stretch of intermediate "thinking" tokens, then the final answer. The extra tokens give it room to try approaches, check steps and backtrack. Spending more computation at answer time, not only during training, is the main idea.

**What it helps:** multi-step math and logic, planning, debugging code, analysis with several constraints, tasks where a first guess is often wrong.
**What it doesn't help much:** simple lookups, rewriting, formatting, friendly chat. Here it only adds delay and cost.

**What it changes for you:**
- **A dial.** Many tools let you set how much the model thinks (a level, or a budget of thinking tokens). More thinking usually means better results on hard tasks, slower replies and a bigger bill.
- **Cost per task.** Thinking tokens are generally billed even when you don't see them. A reasoning model can cost far more per answer than its per-token price suggests (lesson 6.5).
- **Prompts change.** You rarely need to write "think step by step". Spend your words on the goal, constraints and what "correct" means.
- **The visible reasoning is not proof.** The thinking text may be summarized or hidden, and research suggests it does not always reflect what actually drove the answer. Verify results (lesson 1.6) instead of trusting a convincing explanation.
- **Reliability still matters.** A model that solves a hard task 60% of the time is not an automation you can leave alone. Ask what rate of success you need, and test for it (lesson 5.5).

## Example
Task: "Schedule six meetings across three rooms with these constraints." With little thinking the model produces a plausible schedule that double-books a room. With more thinking it finds the conflict and fixes it, taking longer and using several times the tokens.

## Puzzle: Dial it in
Given 8 tasks (some trivial, some hard) and a cost budget, choose a thinking level for each. A simulator reports accuracy, time and cost per task.
- Goal: reach an overall accuracy target at or under the budget.
- Hidden tests: the 8 tasks and 20 simulated runs per task, so you see the success rate rather than one lucky result.
- Grader: simulator totals; feedback shows where extra thinking was wasted and where it was missing.

## Hints
1. Which tasks have an obvious right answer on the first try?
2. Compare success rates across runs, not single runs.

## Reflect
Which of your tasks deserve extra thinking time, and which only need a quick answer?
