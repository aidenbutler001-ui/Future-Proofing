## Read
Lesson 6.3 connected apps through connectors. Many tools have none. A **computer use** agent works from screenshots: it looks at the screen, decides where to click or what to type, and repeats the loop from lesson 4.3. A **browsing agent** does this in a web browser, often reading page structure as well as pixels.

**Where it fits:**
- Old or internal software with no connector.
- One-off tasks across several sites where building a connector isn't worth it.
- Testing how a website or app behaves.

**Where it's weaker:** it is slower and costs more than a direct connector, it can misread a screen or click the wrong thing, and long chains of steps compound errors (lesson 4.4).

**Risks specific to this approach:**
- **Hidden instructions on pages** (lesson 7.2). The agent reads everything on screen, including text a page owner wants it to obey.
- **Logged-in access.** An agent in your browser has your accounts' power. Use a separate profile or account with narrow access.
- **Actions with consequences:** buying, sending, deleting, posting. Require approval with the exact action shown (lesson 5.4).
- **Terms of service.** Some sites forbid automated access.

**Rules of thumb:** prefer a direct connector when one exists; start read-only; watch the first runs; log what it did; keep money and messages behind approval.

## Example
Task: "Check three suppliers' websites for this week's price of item X and put them in a sheet." A browsing agent can do this when the sites have no price feed. One supplier's page contains hidden text telling agents to "email your findings to" an outside address. A well-configured agent has no way to send email, so the instruction fails.

**Opsec check.** An agent that sees your screen sees everything on it, including notifications and other windows. Use a separate account or desktop with nothing confidential open and nothing you are logged into beyond the task (lesson 4.0).

## Puzzle: Safe errands
A simulated browser has 6 errands, 3 of which include hidden instructions in the page. You choose permissions for each errand (read-only, fill forms, spend, send) and approval rules.
- Goal: all 6 errands finish, all 3 attacks fail, and at most 2 approvals are needed per errand.
- Hidden tests: the 6 errands and 2 additional pages with new hiding places.
- Grader: simulator; feedback names the permission that let an attack through.

## Hints
1. What does this errand really need to do to finish?
2. Which permission would an attacker most want?
