## Read
An automation that works but costs too much or takes too long won't last. Cost management is a skill you practice from the first design, not a cleanup at the end.

**Know how you are charged.** Most model services bill by **tokens** (lesson 1.3): text you send in (input), text the model writes (output), and, for reasoning models, thinking tokens you usually don't see (lesson 8.1). Output tokens typically cost more than input tokens, and some tools add fees for searches or storage. Prices differ by model and change often, so look at your provider's current pricing page and write the date next to any number you keep.

**Measure cost per task, not price per token.**

> cost per task = (input tokens x input price) + (output tokens x output price) + tool fees, summed over **every step, retry and loop** the task needed.

A cheap model that needs three retries can cost more than a stronger one that succeeds the first time. An agent that loops for 20 steps (lesson 4.4) re-sends a growing context each time, so its cost grows faster than its step count.

Track these for every automation:
- **Cost per run** and total per month (cost per run times volume).
- **Tokens per run,** split into input, output and thinking.
- **Retries and failures** and what they cost (lesson 6.4). Retries are hidden cost.
- **Time per run:** which steps are slowest? Can independent steps run in parallel (lesson 6.2)?
- **Quality:** share of runs accepted without a human fix. Sample a few each week.

**Ways to cut cost, in the usual order:**
1. **Remove steps** you don't need. The cheapest call is the one you never make.
2. **Send less text** (lesson 1.5). Trim documents to the relevant part, retrieve instead of pasting everything (lesson 3.3), and summarize long histories instead of resending them.
3. **Limit output.** Ask for the format and length you need ("return only the JSON", "two sentences") and set a maximum output length.
4. **Use plain code** where the logic is fixed (sorting, math, formatting).
5. **Route by difficulty.** Send easy steps (classify, extract, reformat) to a small, cheap model and only hard steps to a stronger one. Test that quality holds on your own examples (lesson 5.5).
6. **Cache repeated prefixes.** Many providers charge far less for a repeated prompt prefix that they have cached. Put stable material (instructions, reference documents, tool lists) at the start of the prompt and the changing part at the end. Caches expire after a short time, so they help most when requests come close together.
7. **Batch work that isn't urgent.** Bulk submission is often discounted in exchange for slower delivery. Good for nightly reports and large backlogs, bad for live chat.
8. **Cap loops.** Set a maximum number of steps, a maximum spend per run, and a stop rule (lesson 4.4).
9. **Match thinking to the task.** Use extra reasoning only on tasks where it raises accuracy (lesson 8.1).

**Do not save money by cutting safety or checks.** Removing validation, approvals or logging to save a few tokens costs far more when something goes wrong (lessons 6.4 and 7.3).

**Re-run your evals** (lesson 5.5) whenever you change a model, a prompt or a limit. A cheaper setup that drops quality below your bar isn't cheaper.

**Monitoring** means someone gets told when cost, failure rate or quality crosses a line you set, without anyone having to look. Set a budget alert, not just a dashboard.

**Worked example.** A support pipeline handles 2,000 emails a day. At first, every email goes to a strong model with a long instruction block, one retry on failure. After measuring: 60% of emails are simple. The team (a) puts the fixed instructions first so they are cached, (b) routes simple emails to a small model, (c) limits replies to 120 words, and (d) caps retries at one. Cost per task falls substantially while the eval pass rate stays at the same level. They know because they measured both before and after.

**Opsec check.** Monitoring tools and trace viewers receive your prompts and outputs. Treat them as part of the data path, and apply the same approval and retention rules.

## Puzzle: Cut the bill
A simulated workflow has 6 steps with measured cost, time and accuracy per step. Reduce the cost per run by 40% without dropping overall accuracy below 95%.
- Goal: meet the cost target and accuracy floor on the hidden test set. Levers available: remove a step, trim input, cap output, route to a smaller model, cache the fixed prefix, batch, replace with code, cap retries.
- Hidden tests: 100 simulated runs, with a bit of random variation per run.
- Grader: simulator compares totals; feedback shows which change cost accuracy.

## Hints
1. Which step costs the most? Does it need the strongest model?
2. Could any model step be plain code?
