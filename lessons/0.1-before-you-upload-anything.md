## Read
You have already tried an assistant in the on-ramp. Before you do more with it, this lesson sets the rules for what you share. Every later lesson asks you to give a model text, files or access. **Once you send something, you cannot take it back.** It may be stored, logged, reviewed by staff, or used to improve the service, depending on the product and plan. Even if the provider is trustworthy, your copy now exists in more places, and a mistake or breach can expose it.

**Step 1. Classify what you have.**

| Class | Examples | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Published articles, public web pages | Fine to use |
| **Internal** | Draft plans, class notes, ordinary work email | Only in tools your school or employer approves |
| **Sensitive** | Client files, unpublished research, contracts under NDA, source code, financials, other people's personal details | Only in a tool approved *for that data*, with the right to share it |
| **Restricted** | Health records, student records, government IDs, passwords, keys, security details, anything regulated or classified | **Do not put it in a general AI tool.** Use only an approved, purpose-built system, or none |

**Step 2. Ask four questions before you send.**
1. **Am I allowed?** Employer policy, school policy, a contract, or a law may forbid it, even if the tool would work.
2. **Is this tool approved for this class of data?** A personal free account is not a company-approved system. Work and personal accounts have different rules.
3. **Where does it go, and for how long?** Check retention, whether it is used for training, who can read it, and where it is stored.
4. **Can I get the same result with less?** Remove names, numbers and identifiers; summarize; paste the one paragraph, not the whole file.

**Step 3. Strip it before you send.**
- Replace names and identifiers with labels ("Client A").
- Delete account numbers, addresses, emails, phone numbers, ID numbers.
- **Check hidden data in files:** author names and file paths in the properties, tracked changes, comments, hidden sheets and rows, embedded images, and the filename itself.
- Never include passwords, keys, tokens or recovery codes. They belong in a password manager, not in a prompt.

**Hard rules to remember:**
- **When unsure, don't send it. Ask first.** Asking costs a minute. A leak can't be undone.
- **Never upload a sensitive or restricted document to a tool you haven't checked,** including AI sites you found through a search or an ad. Fake AI sites and extensions exist to harvest what you upload.
- **Never connect a tool to an entire drive, mailbox or computer** when one folder or one document is enough.
- **Don't let AI note-takers join meetings** without everyone's knowledge and agreement.
- **Everything you send may be in a log or history.** Delete what you can; assume you can't delete it all.

## Example
A student wants a summary of a professor's unpublished draft paper that was shared in confidence. Uploading it to a personal AI account would break that confidence, even though the tool is "just a summarizer". The right move: don't upload it, or ask the professor and use an approved tool.

- Goal: the correct class and decision on 7 of 8, with no sensitive item left after redaction.
- Hidden tests: the 8 items; one hides sensitive data in metadata, and one is safe as is (marking it unsafe counts against you).
- Grader: rules detect remaining sensitive strings and metadata and check the decision; feedback names what you missed.
