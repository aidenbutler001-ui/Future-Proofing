## Read
Lesson 1.1 listed the kinds of models. This lesson is the on-ramp for the non-text ones. This course focuses on language models, and these areas are planned as add-on components later.

**Reading images (vision).** Many assistants accept a photo, screenshot, chart or scanned page and answer questions about it. Uses: extracting a table from a screenshot, describing a diagram, reading handwriting, checking a form. Tips: give the model a clear, well-lit image, say what you want from it, and check numbers it reads against the original, since it can misread small text.

**Making images.** You describe what you want and the model draws it. Good prompts name the subject, setting, style, composition and what to avoid, and you improve them by revising from what you see (lesson 2.5). Text inside generated images and fine details like hands are common weak points.

**Speech.** Speech-to-text turns audio into a transcript (meetings, lectures, voice notes). Text-to-speech reads text aloud. Some systems handle voice end to end for natural conversation. Check names and numbers in transcripts.

**Video and audio generation.** Models can create short clips and music from descriptions or images. Quality is rising quickly; clips are short and control is limited.

**Combining them in workflows (lesson 6.2).** Record a meeting, transcribe it, extract action items with a language model, then create calendar entries. Each step is a box in the pipeline.

**Risks:**
- **Realistic fakes.** Generated voices, faces and clips can be used to deceive. Be careful about what you trust, and use independent confirmation for anything important.
- **Consent and likeness.** Don't copy a real person's voice or face without permission.
- **Copyright and style** (lesson 7.4).
- **Provenance.** Label generated media clearly; some tools attach origin information to files.

## Example
A student photographs a whiteboard after a study session. A vision model reads it, a language model turns it into a clean outline, and the student checks two formulas against the textbook because one symbol was misread.

**Opsec check.** Photos and recordings carry more than you intend: faces, screens, documents in the background, location data. Check what is visible and audible before uploading, and get consent from people who appear or speak.

## Puzzle: Image brief
Write a brief for an image generator to match a target description (shown to you), then revise it twice based on the simulated results.
- Goal: the third result matches at least 4 of 5 listed requirements (subject, setting, style, composition, a thing to avoid).
- Hidden tests: two different target descriptions.
- Grader: rubric checks each requirement against the simulated image description; feedback names the requirement your brief didn't express.

## Hints
1. What would a stranger need to know to draw this?
2. Change one thing per revision.

## Reflect
Where would a picture or a voice make your work with AI easier, and where would it make it easier to be fooled?
