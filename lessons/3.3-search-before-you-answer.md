## Read
Your collection might be thousands of pages, far more than any context window. **Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)** handles it in two stages:

1. **Find:** search the collection for the pieces most relevant to the question.
2. **Answer:** place those pieces in the context and ask the model to answer from them (lesson 3.1).

How the finding works:
- Documents are cut into **chunks** (a few paragraphs each).
- Each chunk is turned into an **embedding**: a list of numbers that captures its meaning (the embedding models from lesson 1.1). Chunks about similar things get similar numbers.
- Your question gets an embedding too, and the closest chunks are returned. This is search by meaning, not only by exact words. Many systems also combine it with ordinary keyword search.

Retrieval is the first and most common kind of **tool** a model uses (Module 4). The model, or the harness for it, calls a search function and reads what comes back.

Choices that matter: chunk size (too small loses context, too big dilutes it), how many chunks to return, and whether to include the source name with each chunk.

## Example
Question: "What's our refund window for annual plans?" The search returns the two chunks from the billing policy that mention annual plans and refunds. The model sees only those, plus the question.

**Opsec check.** A search index of your documents is a second copy of them. Decide who can search it and what each searcher may see. Retrieval must respect permissions: a person who cannot open a file must not get its contents through the assistant. Keep the index in an approved location, and delete it when the project ends.

## Puzzle: Tune the search
A simulated knowledge base has 30 chunks. You set chunk size (small, medium, large) and number of results (1 to 8), then run 6 hidden questions. A readout shows which chunks were retrieved.
- Goal: the right chunk is retrieved for at least 5 of 6 questions without returning more than 4 chunks.
- Hidden tests: 6 questions, including one phrased with different words than the document uses and one whose answer spans two chunks.
- Grader: rules check whether the needed chunk is in the results and count the chunks returned.
