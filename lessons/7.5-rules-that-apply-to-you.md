## Read
Few rules are about AI alone. Most already existing rules apply to AI use:

- **Privacy and data protection law** (lesson 7.1) applies to personal data you send to a model.
- **Sector rules** such as health, finance, education and employment may restrict automated decisions.
- **Contracts and confidentiality** with clients and employers can forbid sharing material with outside services.
- **Institutional policies:** university academic integrity policies, company acceptable use policies, and platform terms.
- **AI-specific laws** are arriving in some regions. The European Union's AI Act is the best known; it sorts uses into risk levels and sets duties such as transparency and staff AI literacy. Which duties apply, and from when, depends on the role you play and the use. Timelines have changed and may change again.

You don't need to be a lawyer. You need a short routine:
1. **Name the use** and the people affected.
2. **Check three sources:** your organization's policy, the contracts involved, and the law that applies where you operate.
3. **When in doubt, ask** the right person (compliance, instructor, counsel) before you build, not after.
4. **Write it down:** what the system does, what data it uses, who is responsible.

For universities and companies adopting the course or the tools it teaches, this is the lesson that becomes your internal policy.

## Puzzle: Write the one-page policy
You get a fictional small company that wants to let staff use AI assistants and one automation. Write a one-page AI use policy: allowed uses, data rules, approval rules, who is accountable, and how incidents are reported.
- Goal: cover all 6 required topics, with rules a new employee could follow, and resolve 4 hidden test situations correctly using only your policy.
- Hidden tests: 4 situations (a staff member wants to paste customer records; the automation errs; a new tool is requested; a regulator asks what the system does).
- Grader: rubric and situation checks. Feedback names the missing rule.

## Hints
1. Could a new hire follow this without asking you?
2. What does the policy say to do when something goes wrong?
