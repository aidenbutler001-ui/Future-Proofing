## Read
Models come in sizes. **Small models** are cheaper and faster and some run on a laptop or phone. **Large models** are usually better at hard reasoning and rare knowledge. Where the model runs also matters:

| | Hosted (cloud) | Local (your device) |
|---|---|---|
| Capability | Highest available | Usually lower, improving |
| Privacy | Data goes to a provider (7.1) | Data stays with you |
| Cost | Pay per use | Hardware cost, then low per use |
| Effort | Almost none | Setup and maintenance |

**Open weights vs. open source.** "Open" models let you download and run the trained weights. The license may still limit use, and the training data and code may not be shared. Read the license for what you plan to do.

**Making models fit.** **Quantization** stores a model's numbers with fewer bits so it needs less memory, usually with a small loss in quality. It is why a large model can run on modest hardware (details in the fundamentals track).

**Judge by cost per task, not price per token.** The bill depends on tokens per task times the price, plus retries. Reasoning and agents use many more tokens per task (lesson 8.1). Levers that cut cost:
- **Caching:** repeated parts of a prompt (a long system prompt or document) are cheaper to reuse.
- **Batching:** non-urgent work submitted in bulk is often discounted.
- **Routing:** send easy steps to a small model and only hard ones to a large one (lesson 6.5).
- **Shorter context** (lesson 1.5).

**Price trends.** For a fixed level of capability, prices have fallen quickly. Prices for the newest frontier models have not fallen the same way, and total spending can still rise because agents use so many tokens. Plan for cost per task, and don't assume tomorrow's prices.

**Choosing:** private data, high volume or simple jobs favor small or local models; hard reasoning and rare knowledge favor large ones. Test on your own examples, not on a public leaderboard.

## Example
A clinic wants to summarize patient notes. Sending notes to an outside service isn't allowed, so it runs a small model locally for summaries and sends only anonymized, non-clinical drafting jobs to a hosted model.

**Opsec check.** Running a model locally helps with privacy only if the rest of the setup is safe: download from trusted sources, keep the machine updated and encrypted, and check that no tool in the stack sends data out.

## Puzzle: Right-size it
You get 6 tasks, each with a privacy level, daily volume, difficulty and a monthly budget. Pick a model class and location for each, and a cost lever (cache, batch, route) where it helps.
- Goal: every task meets its privacy rule and budget, and quality stays above the stated floor in the simulator.
- Hidden tests: the 6 tasks plus a change in volume that breaks one choice.
- Grader: simulator totals; feedback shows the cost per task.
