## Read
You met **tokens** in lesson 1.3: the chunks a model reads. A token is roughly three quarters of an English word, so 100 words is about 130 tokens. Everything the model can use for one reply must fit in its **context window**: your messages, its earlier replies, the system prompt, and any documents you attach.

Two ideas follow:

- **Models don't remember between chats by default.** If it seems to remember, the app is putting saved notes or past messages back into the context for you.
- **Space is limited and has a cost.** Long contexts are slower and more expensive, and details in the middle of a very long input are easier to miss. Put what matters clearly and early, and cut what doesn't.

## Example
You paste a 60-page report and ask for the three risks. If the report is longer than the window, the tool may silently cut part of it. Your "three risks" could come only from the first half.

**Opsec check.** Everything in the context window is sent to the provider and may be logged. Long conversations accumulate sensitive details; start a fresh chat for new topics and don't keep sensitive material in a conversation you reuse (lesson 0.1).

## Puzzle: Fit the facts in the budget
You get a pile of 12 short notes and a budget of 150 tokens (shown by a live counter). A question is displayed, such as "When should the team ship, and what could block it?" Choose and trim notes so the context includes the needed facts and stays under budget. Then a simulated model answers using only what you included.

- Goal: the answer includes the ship date and the blocker, within budget.
- Hidden tests: two different questions over the same notes, so you can't just pick one set.
- Grader: rules. Count tokens; check that required facts are present.

## Hints
1. Which notes are about the question and which only sound related?
2. Shorten a note without dropping its fact.
